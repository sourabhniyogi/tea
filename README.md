# Replicate Teactivator results outside UCDavis using UCKearney cultivars
## A practical guide to tissue culture, cuttings, and a fair comparison

> **Status: research and shopping guide, not an approved lab recipe.** This is our AI-assisted reading of the published [AcTEAvator application](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024145233A1/en), not instructions supplied or validated by its inventors, including [Jacquelyn Gervay-Hague](https://chemistry.ucdavis.edu/people/jacquelyn-gervay-hague). Several details must be resolved before the tissue-culture experiment begins. A qualified laboratory supervisor must approve the complete protocol, equipment, chemical safety, and disposal procedures. Nothing in this document claims that UC1 has been tested or that plants will be field-ready in five months.

## 1. The question

We have **42 young tea plants from UC Kearney** and plan to take **one cutting from each plant: 42 cuttings total, with 21 assigned to condition A and 21 to condition B**. **UC1 is the dominant group; the other plants belong to roughly 10 additional variants.**

This is a **mixed-cultivar pilot**, not a UC1-only experiment. We want to compare a tissue-culture approach related to AcTEAvator with ordinary stem cuttings for producing **living, rooted, acclimated tea plants**. Compare outcomes with cultivar identity retained, and report UC1 results separately rather than treating pooled results as evidence for UC1.

**A cutting** is a piece of a plant that can grow into a new plant. **Tissue culture** grows small pieces of a plant, called *explants*, in clean containers on prepared nutrients. An **acclimated plant** has made the transition from a protected culture or rooting environment to normal nursery conditions.

```text
                    OUR STARTING POINT: 42 TEA DONOR PLANTS
                                    │
               ┌────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
               ▼                                          ▼
       A. TISSUE CULTURE (21)                     B. STEM CUTTINGS (21)
  clean shoot → culture vessels             cutting → rooting hormone
     → multiply shoots                      → rooting medium → roots
               │                                          │
       root + acclimate                            acclimate to nursery
               └────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                    ▼
              Count healthy, rooted nursery-ready plants
                    + measure time, labor and cost
```

This compares **two complete production systems**, not just two hormones. AcTEAvator includes several stages, while ordinary cuttings typically do not. The initial pilot has two conditions only.

## 2. What AcTEAvator is trying to do

The published application (Gervay-Hague and Tricoli; applicants: University of California, Berkeley and University of California, San Diego; published 4 July 2024) describes multiple media and steps for growing tea shoots and producing leaves in culture. A medium is the food-and-water mixture that supports a cultured plant; **agar** makes a medium gel-like. The precise medium changes as the plant tissue progresses.

The roman numerals below are the patent's own step labels: claim 1 gives (i)–(iii), claim 30 adds (iv), and claim 39 adds (v). Steps (iv) and (v) are additions in dependent claims, not requirements of claim 1 alone. Claim 41 ties the five-month statement to (i)–(v). Preparation is before (i); rooting is a separate, unnumbered addition in claim 42, not step (v). [Source: claims 1, 3, 30, 39, 41 and 42](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024145233A1/en).

```text
Preparation (before i; not a numbered stage)
    ↓
(i) Budding
    ↓
(ii) Shoot multiplication
    ↓
(iii) Shoot elongation
    ↓
(iv) Optimized growth (added by claim 30)
    ↓
(v) Leaf harvest (added by claim 39)

Our nursery endpoint: rooting + acclimation, not leaf harvest.
Rooting is unnumbered in claim 42; the lab must define our plant pathway.
```

**A leaf harvest is not the same thing as a potted plant.** The publication's "about five months" runs from step (i), placing a shoot on budding medium, to step (v), *harvesting a top bud and a first set of leaves* (paragraphs 0032 and 0092; claim 41). Rooting appears only as an optional extra step ("rooting the shoot cutting before placing it into soil," paragraphs 0035 and 0093; claim 42). Example 1 (paragraphs 0108–0111) does not describe rooting or acclimation at all. Five months is therefore **not** evidence that a rooted plant is ready for a field in five months. We need Jackie's recommended rooting and acclimation steps and a definition of "nursery-ready" before comparing results.

## 3. Design a fair pilot

### Before taking any cuttings

**Get an SOP or improve this document with Jackie Hague's supervision, then acquire materials for the above.**

**Fixed pilot allocation: 42 donors → 42 cuttings → 21 A + 21 B.** Take one cutting from each donor plant, subject to the lab confirming that the young plants can safely provide suitable material. Each donor contributes to **one condition only**, not both. Jackie and the lab should approve suitability and handling without silently changing the planned allocation.

Before collection, make an inventory with donor IDs P01–P42, each plant's label/cultivar, and the planned condition. Verify the provisional 40-plant breakdown and the remaining two plants; record any uncertain identity explicitly.

- Match or stratify the assignment by cultivar wherever possible. For a cultivar represented by two plants, randomly assign one distinct donor to A and the other to B, for example by coin flip. This is a pair of different donor plants, not two cuttings from one donor.
- For larger cultivar groups, randomize donors into balanced groups. If the provisional 20 UC1 count is confirmed, allocate 10 UC1 donors to each condition.
- Resolve the remaining two plants' identities before final assignment and balance cultivar representation as far as possible while retaining exactly 21 donors per condition. Record any unavoidable imbalance or unknown identity; do not invent a cultivar label.
- Label the starting cuttings A01–A21 and B01–B21 and link each to its unique donor and cultivar. If a cutting yields multiple tissue pieces or daughter shoots, use linked suffixes and do not count them as additional independent starting cuttings.

This is a **feasibility pilot**, not a basis for strong cultivar-specific conclusions. A cultivar with only two plants contributes just one donor per condition, which cannot separate treatment effects from donor differences. Report UC1 separately and retain cultivar-level records for all others. Differences in cultivar, donor health, tissue age, season, handling or contamination may determine the outcome; donor matching by cultivar does not make different donor plants identical. Products of one original cutting are not independent starting replicates. Expand the pilot only after the first run and the lab's review.

### Follow both paths

**A — Culture system:** With trained supervision, establish, multiply and grow the 21 assigned starting cuttings using the lab-approved approach; then follow an agreed root-and-acclimate procedure. Record failed establishment, contamination, shoots created, losses during rooting and acclimation, and time. Keep the parent ID linked to every resulting plant.

**B — Conventional cuttings:** Use the 21 assigned cuttings using a tea-propagator-approved method and a labeled rooting product suitable for the use. Keep moisture, shade and growing conditions consistent **within this group**. Record the number started, rooted, lost and acclimated. Photograph regularly without disturbing roots unnecessarily.

**Known limitations to state in any report.**

- The two groups start with different kinds of material (a small sterilized shoot versus a conventional cutting) and take different amounts of time; compare **time to endpoint**, not results on one calendar date.
- There is no "no-hormone" control in either group, so the results cannot say whether the hormones, the medium or the handling made the difference.
- Each donor contributes only one cutting to one condition, so this is not a within-donor comparison. Cultivar-balanced randomization reduces imbalance but cannot remove individual donor differences.
- Mixed-cultivar pooled results may hide cultivar-specific responses. Report UC1 separately; one donor per condition for the paired variants is insufficient for cultivar-specific conclusions.
- Season and greenhouse conditions affect rooting of cuttings; record start dates and conditions.
- The pilot cannot detect genetic changes that tissue culture can occasionally cause, and it does not measure leaf yield or tea quality.

### E. Lab safety rules

- Work only in the approved lab, under a qualified lab supervisor, after safety training.
- **Only trained, authorized personnel prepare or handle**: silver nitrate (an oxidizer, which stains skin and needs hazardous-waste disposal), any silver thiosulfate stock, PPM (a biocide), concentrated hormone stocks, bleach solutions, autoclaves and scalpels.
- Wear gloves and eye protection; chlorophenol red is a skin and eye irritant.
- Never mix bleach with acids or ammonia.
- Do not eat or drink experimental plants or media, and follow the lab's disposal rules for contaminated cultures and silver waste.
